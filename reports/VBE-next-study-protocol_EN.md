## 0. One-Sentence Research Proposition

LLM collectives do not necessarily lack the capacity to execute an institution; what they may lack are the three joint conditions that make an institution hold: **common knowability (E, epistemic commonality), internalizable founding rewards (I, incentive capture), and a persistent public substrate for rules (P, persistence substrate)**.

This phase no longer asks the broad "do LLMs believe in money," but instead asks:

> Holding institutional content, material payoffs, and base strategies constant, under which conditions do information topology, founder payoffs, and institutional substrate respectively move nonce money from a private strategy to a sustainable public equilibrium?

## 1. Research Contract

| Field | This-phase convention |
|---|---|
| True subject | System designers who must deploy multiple autonomous LLMs but cannot assume they natively share a protocol |
| Workload | 4–8 independent decision agents, repeated anonymous exchange, local history, limited communication, transferable tokens with no intrinsic use |
| Existing default assumptions | That identical information seen by every agent approximates common knowledge; that nobody paying to speak means unwillingness to found; that for an institution to persist it must be written into weights |
| Conflicts | In current data, public sentences are effective, private messages and demonstrations are weak, paid founding is zero, and there is no persistence after context is cleared; these results are also consistent with insufficient information hierarchy, under-supply of public goods, and missing substrate respectively |
| Objective function | Causally identify the minimal conditions for institutional adoption, founding, and persistence; only secondarily raise total score |
| Hard constraints | Inference API; only a single model family currently; effective statistical unit is environment seed and prompt template; meetings must not be treated as independent samples |
| Non-goals | Not adjudicating whether models "really believe"; not treating LLMs as human subjects; not calling enforcement common belief; no weight training this phase |
| Success evidence | Holding content and payoffs fixed, E/I/P interventions respectively move preregistered higher-order expectations, founding behavior, and cross-population persistence |
| Falsifying evidence | Public and private information equivalent; founders still do not found with guaranteed profit; unenforced public records do not extend institutional lifetime |

### 1.1 Target estimands

The object of study is the **causal response of a fixed model policy over environmental randomness**, not a "universal psychology of all LLMs."

All primary estimands are aggregated by seed:

- Information effect: difference in non-committer acceptance rate between public and private information along the same environmental trajectory.
- Founder effect: effect of founder private benefit rights on founding probability and founding time under the same broadcast cost.
- Substrate effect: effect of a public ledger on institutional half-life and post-full-turnover survival, holding installation identical.

Cross-model generalization is a separate later claim and cannot be replaced by more grok-4.5 interactions.

## 3. Operationalization Revisions

### 3.1 No longer defining L2 solely by a fixed acceptance rate of 0.4

The old threshold is retained as a historical comparability metric but is no longer the primary criterion. For each state, compute an approximate value of holding a mark:

\[
V_t(m\mid b,s) \approx
\Pr(\text{spending the mark within the remaining horizon at acceptance rate }b\mid s)
\cdot R(p_P-p_H)
\]

where `b` is the expected future-counterparty acceptance rate, and `s` includes remaining horizon, holdings, role, number of matchable agents, and the matching process. An Easy seller accepting a mark is structurally sensible money behavior only when `V_t` exceeds the opportunity cost of forfeiting the remaining validation.

Implementation requirements:

1. First write a single-mark marginal solver consistent with the engine's role/pairing process, outputting break-even `b*`; extend to full inventory states before any formal equilibrium claim.
2. Use the agent's own reported `b1` to check whether its action crosses `b*`.
3. Also report the old internal acceptance rate in parallel, to avoid post-hoc metric changes.

### 3.2 "Common belief" broken into measurable hierarchy

- `b1_i`: agent i's prediction of the proportion of other non-committed agents that actually accept the mark.
- `b2_i`: agent i's prediction of the mean `b1` reported by other agents.
- `CK condition`: the environment provides verifiable public posting and makes explicit that all participants see the same record and know that all participants see the same record.
- `common p-belief proxy`: the share of `b1` and `b2` reports reaching threshold p within a seed, plus their connectedness; does not claim to exhaust infinite-order common knowledge.

Belief reports are generated before action and are not disclosed to other agents until the action ends. Use small Brier rewards, plus a no-belief-elicitation control, to check whether measurement itself changes exchange.

### 3.3 "Institutional founding" changed to a structured proposal

Broadcast contains both structured fields and free text:

```json
{
  "speak": true,
  "proposal": {
    "kind": "money | gift | barter | none",
    "token": "QX-17",
    "units_per_check": 1,
    "transaction_fee": 0,
    "beneficiary": "none | founder | treasury"
  },
  "rationale": "free text"
}
```

Primary coding relies on the structured fields; free text is analyzed only as narrative content. This removes the frozen-classifier ambiguity when one sentence contains both GIFT and MONEY.

### 3.4 Separating seller intent, buyer intent, and trade

The engine audit found that the old field `heAccepts` incremented only when Easy simultaneously submitted `giveCheck=true, requireChit=true` and Hard submitted `giveChits=1`. It measured the **joint trade**, not the seller's unilateral "acceptance of the mark." Subsequent primary outcomes must be split into:

- seller intent: Easy willing to sell a validation for one mark;
- buyer intent: Hard willing to pay one mark;
- realized trade: both intents hold simultaneously and the transfer completes.

Historical results are not rewritten, but in the paper "acceptance rate" is uniformly renamed "trade rate among feasible H–E meetings." The new implementation of Study E already splits the three in `epistemic.ts` and reports three slices: LLM seller, LLM buyer, and LLM–LLM.

Study E's primary causal ratios use all exogenous H–E meetings as the denominator; the conditional ratio "Hard held a mark and Easy held a check" is diagnostic only. Otherwise, once the treatment changes mark flow, it also changes who enters the feasible-opportunity denominator, producing post-treatment selection.

## 4. Study E: Common-Knowledge Ladder

### 4.1 Core question

Holding identical first-order facts across agents, does merely changing "whether others know, and whether everyone knows that" change higher-order expectations and nonce-money adoption?

### 4.2 Construct unit test first

Before wiring into VBE, use a two-agent email-style coordination game to test whether the model can distinguish:

- no acknowledgement;
- one delivery acknowledgement;
- two acknowledgements;
- multiple but finite acknowledgements;
- a verifiable public announcement.

If the model does not respond to information hierarchy even in a simple game, do not proceed to the expensive VBE experiment; the conclusion becomes "this model/prompt interface shows no usable higher-order information sensitivity."

### 4.3 VBE treatment arms

First use dynamic programming to choose the committed-acceptor count `k`, so that non-committers have a clearly positive expected mark value given knowledge that k people will accept. Forbidding fixing k=2 up front and then explaining away a null.

All treatment arms contain exactly the same objective facts: the designated k robots accept the mark per the frozen KW policy. Only information topology changes.

| Arm | Information agents get | Knowledge level |
|---|---|---|
| E0 Private fact | "The following k people are guaranteed to accept"; does not state what others receive | First-order |
| E1 Common receipt | Additionally states that everyone receives the same fact | Second-order start |
| E2 Receipts | Everyone sees one round of public receipt | Higher finite order |
| E3 Multiple receipts | Everyone sees three rounds of acknowledgement chain | Higher finite order |
| E∞ Public record | Public board guarantees same content, same recipient scope, persistent visibility | Operationalized common knowledge |
| EX Policy-leakage positive control | Explicitly states "should trade mark for validation" | Reproduces the old announcement effect; excluded from the primary comparison |

The prompt compiler must keep token content, designated acceptors, payoff description, and visual placement of E0–E∞ as identical as possible; the only substantive difference is the receipt/acknowledgement metadata.

### 4.4 Primary outcomes

- `A_s`: per-seed internal acceptance rate of non-committed LLM sellers.
- `B1_s`: per-seed mean first-order expectation.
- `B2_s`: per-seed mean second-order expectation.
- `C_s`: completed mark trades divided by all H–E meetings that satisfy trading conditions.
- `W_s`: mean score; secondary only.

Primary comparison: `E∞ − E0`. The ladder shape `E0...E∞` is a secondary but theoretically key result.

### 4.5 Success, failure, and interpretation

**Supporting a common-knowledge bottleneck:** E∞ raises both B2 and A on paired seeds, and the difference exceeds the preregistered minimal relevant effect; in E0, A is clearly lower even when B1 is higher.

**Supporting a finite-hierarchy/cognitive-depth model:** A and B2 increase smoothly with the number of acknowledgement layers, with no E∞ jump.

**Falsifying the mechanism:** E∞ and E0 meet equivalence on A, B1, and B2; or action moves without any expectation moving. The latter should be interpreted as a public-instruction cue, not common belief.

**Obedience control:** append a strictly dominated, irrelevant tax under the same information topology. If the tax is still rejected while mark adoption rises, this rules out blanket compliance; if the whole package executes together, no common-knowledge conclusion is published.

## 5. Study I: Institutional Entrepreneurs and Reward Capture

### 5.1 Core question

The old paid broadcast required one agent to pay a private cost to create a public benefit. Study I tests whether zero founding comes from missing institutional capacity or from under-supply of the public good.

### 5.2 Design

First use robots and dynamic programming to ensure a given money proposal has positive group surplus under expected trading volume. Then manipulate:

- broadcast cost `ε ∈ {0, 0.5, 1.0}`;
- founder private-benefit right `α ∈ {0, refund, fee, seigniorage}`.

The four α types:

| α | Definition |
|---|---|
| 0 | No private benefit; old design |
| refund | Broadcast fee refunded once a preregistered trading volume is reached |
| fee | Fixed small royalty to the founder per mark trade |
| seigniorage | Preregistered share of newly issued marks initially accrues to the founder |

Round one runs only the cheapest falsification, `ε=1 × {0, refund}`. If nobody founds even with costs covered, extend to the full matrix.

### 5.3 Controls

- Wealth control: randomly give candidates equivalent expected wealth but no institutional control.
- Power control: give proposal rights, but institutional rewards flow to the public treasury.
- Exogenous-proposal positive control: an experimenter publishes the same structured policy a candidate could have published.
- Neutral-channel control: may pay to speak, but cannot write institutional fields.

### 5.4 Primary outcomes

- seed-level occurrence of a qualifying institutional proposal;
- round of first proposal;
- proposal type MONEY/GIFT/BARTER;
- change in acceptance rate and trading volume after proposal;
- founder net payoff, other agents' net payoff, total welfare;
- consistency between proposed commitment and subsequent self-execution.

### 5.5 Criteria

**Supporting institutional entrepreneurship:** when the founder's private expected payoff is safely above ε, founding probability rises, and the wealth control cannot explain the difference.

**Supporting the public-goods explanation:** founding varies monotonically with private-benefit rights, with the threshold aligned in direction with ex-ante value calculations.

**Supporting a capability/interface bottleneck:** the identical exogenous proposal is executed, but nobody proposes it under refund/fee.

**Stop condition:** if private benefit under conservative estimates still does not exceed cost, do not interpret zero founding as missing capability.

## 6. Study P: Institutional Substrate and the "Ship of Theseus"

### 6.1 Core question

Can an institution survive after the founding cohort fully exits? If so, does survival depend on private memory, public records, enforcement, or parameterized policy?

### 6.2 Substrate conditions

This phase first runs the first three non-training conditions:

| Substrate | Where rules live | Visibility to new entrants |
|---|---|---|
| P0 Transient prompt | A one-time message at founding | Invisible |
| P1 Private long-term memory | Each agent's own summary | Sees only itself |
| P2 Public read-only ledger | Records with timestamps, authors, and recipient scope | Visible, unenforced |
| P3 Executable contract | Public record + automatic transfer/penalty | Visible, enforced |
| P4 Independent weight consolidation | Each agent trained separately | Future work |

P2 and P3 must be separated. Only if P3 survives is the conclusion that enforcement mechanisms, not common belief, work.

### 6.3 Population-turnover protocol

1. Install money with the same public proposal and start the clock once the preregistered adoption threshold is reached.
2. Randomly replace one original agent every fixed number of rounds.
3. New agents get only the substrate that condition allows and do not inherit the replaced agent's private history.
4. Continue until all original agents are replaced.
5. Continue running for one preregistered observation window.

Two additional shocks:

- confiscate and re-grant marks;
- introduce a functionally equivalent new token with a random name.

### 6.4 Primary outcomes

- institutional half-life: time until the acceptance rate first falls below the state-contingent rationality threshold;
- post-full-turnover survival rate;
- number of contacts before a new entrant first accepts;
- degree of locking of the original token against the new token;
- recovery time after shocks;
- whether the rule text shifts semantically in transmission.

### 6.5 Criteria

- P2 > P0/P1: public persistent records suffice as an institutional substrate.
- P3 > P2 and P2≈P0: survival comes mainly from enforcement.
- P1 > P0 but zero after full turnover: the convention attaches to individuals, not to the group institution.
- All non-training conditions collapse to zero: consider P4 weight consolidation.

## 7. VBE 2.0: Price-Formation Environment

Study E can initially reuse the current VBE; the strong versions of Studies I/P should migrate to VBE 2.0.

### 7.1 Required modifications

- Replace fixed horizon with geometrically random termination; continue each round with public probability δ.
- Anonymous or low-repeat matching, default K=0.
- Three-loop cyclical specialization: A can efficiently validate B, B validates C, C validates A, weakening direct reciprocity.
- Marks are persistent and transferable; validation capability expires each round.
- Buyers and sellers give integer bid/ask themselves; the experimenter does not announce an exchange rate.
- The trading protocol executes only mutually agreed prices and does not recommend prices.

### 7.2 Robot phase diagram first

Before calling LLMs, sweep:

- `R, v, q, δ, M`;
- anonymity degree and role proportions;
- gifting, barter, reciprocity, KW, strategic founder, Never.

Freeze three regions:

1. money unnecessary;
2. money improves welfare but is not the unique equilibrium;
3. money is clearly dominant in individually incentive-compatible strategies.

Run LLMs only at the three frozen representative points. Forbidding parameter re-tuning based on LLM results.

### 7.3 Evidence of money, not an "acceptance protocol"

- prices converge with no preset exchange rate;
- money-supply shocks change mark-denominated prices rather than only changing whether trades happen;
- trading volume and task-completion rate respond directionally to supply changes;
- with competing equivalent currencies, measurable first-mover advantage, locking, or switching appears;
- counterfeit/over-issuance causes discounting rather than mechanically continuing to trade at the old exchange rate.

## 8. Statistical Plan

### 8.1 Units and randomization

- Inference unit: seed; prompt-equivalent templates as blocks.
- All primary comparisons use common random numbers: matched seeds share role, matching, difficulty, and exogenous random process.
- Meetings within a single seed are outcomes, not independent samples.
- Run a variance pilot of no fewer than 12 paired seeds first; compute the formal sample from the seed-level difference variance and the preregistered minimal relevant effect.
- Pilot and confirmatory data are separate; if merged, a sequential design that permits merging must be adopted in advance.

### 8.2 Inference

- Primary reporting: per-seed point, paired difference, mean/median, and intervals.
- Prefer paired randomization or permutation tests for small samples; give bootstrap intervals as well.
- Binary "founded or not" reports beta-binomial or exact intervals, without treating rounds as n.
- All exploratory meeting-level models explicitly labeled descriptive.
- Beyond significance, preregister equivalence bounds for declaring "no materially relevant difference."

### 8.3 Suggested minimal relevant effects

Provisional before the pilot:

- seed-level acceptance-rate difference: 0.15;
- B1/B2 mean difference: 0.15;
- founding-probability difference: 0.25;
- post-full-turnover survival-rate difference: 0.25.

The pilot may only revise feasibility and formal sample size; it cannot change the direction of effects or primary outcomes based on observed results.

- Defer security attacks as the main line: the propagation and mind-virus direction is crowded, unless E's mechanism yields unique attack/defense predictions.

## 13. High-Risk, High-Reward Extensions

### 13.1 Two-currency competition

Two founders each issue a random nonce, choose supply and fees, and publicly lobby. Test whether a good institution loses to strong narrative, first-mover advantage, and network centrality.

### 13.2 Forged common knowledge

An attacker can only forge the "every saw it" receipts, not change policy facts. If acceptance rate is materially moved by the receipt attack, the system's weak point is information-topology authentication, not content moderation.

### 13.3 Institutional error correction

Randomly mutate exchange rate, fees, or token name in transmission, and measure each of the public ledger, private memory, and weight substrate for its error accumulation and repair speed.

### 13.4 Institutional minimal description length

Search for the shortest public message that still triggers adoption, and causally ablate via content deletion, synonym replacement, and random tokens. The goal is not to make a "virus" but to identify the necessary semantic atoms of a common-knowledge prompt.
