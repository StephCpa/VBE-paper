# Paper 1 — Response to the Second Review (2026-10-01)

**Review:** Borderline, leaning Weak Reject (AAMAS 2027).
**Scope of this revision:** manuscript, figures, supplement, engine tests, and a zero-call reanalysis of frozen traces. **No model call was made, and no frozen protocol, prompt, result artifact, gate, or verdict was changed.** All new numbers come from `engine/src/data/welfare-review-x1.json` (generated by `reanalyze-welfare-review-x1.ts`) or from the frozen result files.

The reviewer's diagnosis was that the experiments show credible local effects that are not yet organized into a mechanism study. The revision answers that mainly with existing data. Every frozen run is replayed exactly through the frozen engine, which recovers the prompt behind each of 14,010 decisions and makes an exact welfare identity and a fixed-state comparison possible. Two of the three highest-priority experiments are therefore partly answered without calls; the factorial (A) still requires new calls and is specified as a frozen-ready protocol.

In an E–H meeting, keeping is strictly dominant for both roles. The paper now states that the gift announcement's welfare comes from cooperation in an unnamed Prisoner's Dilemma, net of dominated giving. On the specific concern: an agent that does not give a check can never lose a mark without receiving one. The only mark-for-nothing path is an unconditional giver voluntarily attaching its own marks to a gift, documented as a test.

## Major Concern 2 — Missing 2×2 control

**Not run (requires model calls); specified and sized.** Protocol A (`reports/VBE-paper1-r2-followup-protocols.md`) is the neutral/gift × standard/H–H-blocked factorial on 24 fresh seeds, with the interaction estimand, both simple effects, and an equivalence criterion for any "necessity" claim. The power calculation uses W-RG's observed seed SD. The paper states plainly that W-RG cannot identify the share of the gift-versus-neutral effect carried by H–H (§4.1, Limitations).

In the meantime, the exact accounting identity (zero-call) shows that, in W-SGB, H–H swaps contribute +2.20 per account to the +2.06 gift-minus-neutral difference and one-way H→E gifts −0.62 (Figure 1B). The paper labels this an accounting identity, not a causal decomposition. It also reports that the gift arm with H–H blocked falls below the same seeds' never-transfer benchmark in 10/12 seeds.

- **2.1 — accepted.** The paper now says directly that removing transfers worth 3.66 each must lower welfare. It locates the informative part elsewhere: the removed channel is unnamed, and what remains of the announcement's effect is net wasteful.
- **2.2 — accepted, with the audit requested.** The E→H filter is now described as blocking unconditional E→H *give proposals*. The audit (`filterAudit`) shows its 20 blocked proposals: 16 would have produced E–H swaps and instead executed as one-way H→E gifts, and 4 would have produced one-way E→H gifts and became no transfer. H→E giving was untouched by the filter. The regime contrast is reinterpreted accordingly in §4.1.

## Major Concern 3 — The post-hoc 0.25 screen

**Done.** The screen is removed from all core claims and kept only as a descriptive diagnostic. X1 reports named-relation deltas with seed-level CIs both in closed loop and at first decisions, plus a threshold sweep (0.05–0.30). The sweep shows the concern was justified. Gift exact's named E→H delta is 0.123 in closed loop (clears 0.10, fails 0.15) but 0.482 at first decisions (clears every threshold). The any-holder and Hard-partner packages show the reverse. The conclusion that the named relation is engaged now rests on the fixed-state comparison: 17/0 discordant first decisions in W-SGB, replicated in W-CO (26 of 41) and W-RG (16 of 31). The low closed-loop rate is shown to reflect decay after the first meeting (§4.3), not a failure of comprehension.

## Major Concern 4 — Role-conditioned proposals conflate text effects with state shifts

**Partly answered without calls; the rest specified.** Because the prompt is a pure function of logged state and the paired engine replays exactly, X1 reconstructs every prompt. At each agent's first meeting, the prompt carries no treatment-dependent history and is byte-identical across arms once the announcement is removed (asserted for every first meeting in all three studies). This is the reviewer's "first decision without post-treatment history" condition, obtained from frozen data.

- The harmful E–E directive raises first-decision H→H giving to 0.83 vs 0.39 (8 one-way discordant decisions, 0 the other way), about its full closed-loop effect. The spillover is therefore a direct text effect at a fixed state, not a by-product of the trajectories the directive creates. This is exactly the distinction the review asked to draw.
- First-decision responses map the boundary (Figure 2A, §4.2). Explicit exclusion clauses suppress the excluded relation; positive role predicates do not confine the response; Easy agents give only when the package names Easy actors.
- A temperature-0 noise floor is measured. Byte-identical prompts in W-RG's gift arms agree 87–90% of the time, with symmetric disagreements, so the one-directional splits above are far outside serving noise (cited: Atıl et al. 2025).
- **Remaining.** Cross-announcement replay at later states with identical non-empty histories needs calls (Protocol B, with replicates and paraphrases).
- **Contagion wording — fixed.** "Contagious" is removed. The paper states that every agent receives the announcement directly, so cross-role responses are population responses, not peer-to-peer contagion.

## Major Concern 5 — Money funnel and the harmful directive

- **5.1 — done.** The funnel locates the failure exactly (§4.4, X1 §4). Under money exact: 304 E–H meetings → buyer holds a mark in 140 → seller proposes a sale in 140 → buyer offers a mark in 0. Across all 13 arms of the three studies, Hard agents offered a mark in 0 of 3,443 decisions taken while holding one, including under an announcement telling them they may buy. The failure is buyer non-payment. The ambiguous "Named Δ for Money … E→H column" table is gone.
- **5.2 — not run; specified.** Protocol C is the payoff-salience 2×2 at fixed E–E states, with a non-directive payoff table, a separate-call comprehension probe, endgame strata, and the reviewer's three pre-specified readings. New descriptive evidence: harmful compliance does not decay. E–E giving stays at 0.99 at agents' fourth and later meetings, whereas the named E→H gift collapses after one meeting (§4.3).

## Major Concern 6 — Statistical qualifications

**Done.**

- 6.1: The paper states that passing an MRES gate shows a large estimate and a nonzero effect, not Δ > MRES. Table 5 adds supplementary exact tests of H0: Δ ≤ MRES (Holm within W-SGB families). Every passing frozen gate also passes this stricter test except one, which is reported: the harmful directive's H–H effect (raw p = 0.0089, Holm p = 0.027).
- 6.2: Table notes and §3 state the zero-centered sign-symmetry null. They also note that with 12 or 14 seeds the minimum attainable p is 2^−12 or 2^−14, reached whenever every seed agrees in sign, so sign counts are reported for every contrast.
- 6.3: Tables state that tests are one-sided for Δ > 0 and intervals are pointwise, not simultaneous. Seed-level plots are added: W-RG in Figure 1A, and all 16 W-SGB contrasts in the supplementary figure `figures/VBE-x1-supp-seed-level.pdf`. Leave-one-seed-out ranges keep every passing contrast above its MRES. One correction to the earlier draft: W-RG gift − neutral is 9/12 positive (12/12 applies to the blocking contrasts only).

## Major Concern 7 — Incremental contribution

**Revised.** The introduction now states the contribution as an identification design plus the result it produces. The design combines execution-layer intervention under a fixed prompt, an exact transaction-level welfare identity, fixed-state comparison from frozen logs, and an engaged harmful control. Together they overturn the intuitive explanation (agents do what the rule says) and locate the welfare channel (cooperation in an unnamed Prisoner's Dilemma) and the failure modes (decaying named compliance, persistent harmful compliance, buyer non-payment). Related work now differentiates Concordia (described actions resolved by a game master), GovSim (aggregate outcomes with communication ablations and capability probes), and Welfare Diplomacy without overstating them. We chose a textual differentiation over a ✓/✗ table, to avoid misrepresenting prior designs we cannot audit at the same depth; we would add the table if the venue prefers it.
