## II. The three "mechanistic findings"

### II.1 Endgame cooperation — adopted with the recommended wording

X2 §1. Under gift with standard execution (three studies pooled), H→H giving in the final round (t = 24) is **27/30** decisions. Those come from **15 meetings in 15 seeds**; both agents gave in 13 of them. The rate in rounds 1–21 is 0.91. The final-minus-earlier difference is −0.02, with seed-cluster 95% CI [−0.18, 0.10]. Per study: W-SGB 9/10, W-RG 8/10, W-CO 10/10.

Paper §4.3 now says that high final-round giving with no apparent decline is *inconsistent with explanations that rely entirely on future incentives and final-round best responses*. It also says the data cannot separate misuse of the horizon, misrepresented payoffs, persistence of earlier actions, or an announcement-induced objective. It says the final-round sample is too small to claim a zero endgame effect. The stage-game arithmetic (0.50 and 0.96) is quoted from Table 2. The sentence "agents aren't cooperating to protect future payoffs" does not appear. The decisions are not treated as 30 independent samples: meetings and seeds are reported, and the interval resamples seeds.

### II.2 "The round effect has been ruled out" — not adopted; the exact-round analysis was run

X2 §2. The review's point is confirmed by the data. Round 1 cannot carry history, and the pooled comparison (first meetings in rounds 1–2, 0.51 = 41/81, against prior-meeting decisions, 0.05 = 1/20) mixes round into history. The requested comparisons:

- **Round 2 only:** first meeting 0.45 (13/29) vs prior meeting 0.05 (1/20).
- Rounds 3–6 separately are reported in the X2 table (small cells).
- **Round-standardized risk difference** (Mantel–Haenszel weights, stratified by study and exact round, rounds 2–6): **0.44, seed-cluster CI [0.28, 0.61]**. W-SGB alone: 0.22 [−0.04, 0.56].

The gap survives standardization by exact round in the pooled data. The paper nonetheless uses only the recommended wording: the comparison *narrows* the round confound but does not isolate the memory field, because a prior meeting also changes score, own past action, and prompt length. It points to Protocol B2.

**A new descriptive result sharpens this** (X2 §4, `firstInCell`). An Easy agent's *first E→H decision* gives at **0.06** (13/222; CI [0.03, 0.09]) when it follows a meeting in another role, against 0.53 (59/111) when it is the account's first meeting. The drop in named giving therefore does not require earlier experience of the named relation. The paper reports this as a description, not as evidence of a memory mechanism.

### II.3 "Agents still give after losses" — reworded; the split changes the reading

X2 §3. The proposed figure reproduces: under gift, Hard agents whose previous meeting was an unreciprocated transfer give to their next Hard partner at **0.89 (94/106)**. As the review suspected, that number does not measure retaliation:

| Condition (gift, standard execution, three studies) | Give rate |
|---|---|
| Previous meeting an unreciprocated transfer, in another cell (mostly H→E) | 0.92 (85/92) |
| Previous meeting an unreciprocated H→H transfer, new partner | **0.64 (9/14)** |
| Same partner as the loss (immediately consecutive meetings) | 0.92 (11/12) |
| Last H–H meeting unreciprocated | **0.73 (22/30)** |
| Last H–H meeting mutual | **0.97 (365/377)** |
| Harmful E–E: previous meeting unreciprocated | **0.37 (7/19)** vs 0.83 at first meetings |

Most of the 0.89 comes from losses in H→E meetings. After an unreciprocated H–H transfer, giving in the next H–H meeting is lower. So the blanket claim "cooperation barely depends on exploitation history" would have been wrong. The paper (§4.3) reports the split, notes that random rematching means most rates concern new partners, gives the same-partner count (12), and keeps only the claim the review allows: *under some announcements, giving toward later Hard partners stays high after unrewarded transfers*. No "no retaliation" claim appears.

### II.4 "Named gifts only occur on first encounters" — corrected, denominators unified

X2 §4 reports numerator, denominator, and seed count for the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th+, and all later encounters, under both definitions:

- **Account meeting index** (any role): W-CO 26/41 → 1/45 (second) → 23/332 (all later); W-RG 16/31 → 0/24 → 10/231; W-SGB 17/39 → 2/29 → 20/265.
- **Index within the E→H cell:** W-CO 31/134 → 10/106 → 18/239; W-RG 18/91 → 5/75 → 8/171; W-SGB 23/108 → 7/83 → 14/196.

R2's 0.63 → 0.02, 0.52 → 0.00, 0.44 → 0.07 were first → second meeting under the first definition. The suggested 0.07, 0.04, 0.08 were first → all later meetings. Both are now stated with their denominators. The paper says named giving *drops sharply* after the first meeting and that named gifts still occur late (4/31 final-round E→H decisions).

## III. The two new experiments

Both are specified in `reports/VBE-paper1-r3-followup-protocols.md` (v0.2), which supersedes the r2 protocols.

**A (four-arm factorial).** The estimands are τ_S, τ_B, and I = τ_S − τ_B, reported with intervals rather than only an interaction test. All four arms run on fresh seeds, interleaved and counterbalanced in one service window; no arm is attached to old trajectories. The protocol prespecifies a ±0.75 equivalence bound (TOST) for "no gain under blocking" and a direct one-sided test of τ_B < 0 for "harmful under blocking". The −0.4 prediction is corrected: the data fix Y(G,B) − Y(N,S) = −1.53, but not τ_B. The value τ_B ≈ −0.44 follows only from the auxiliary assumption Y(N,B) ≈ never-transfer and is used only for sizing. With 32 seeds, the sizing formula (seed-level SD priors from W-RG) gives an interaction MDE ≈ 0.89, TOST power ≈ 0.98 at τ_B = 0, and harm-test power ≈ 0.70 at τ_B = −0.44. The budget is ≈ 9,900 calls. The paper (§4.1) states the same algebra.

**B (modular fixed-state study)** is split into four prespecified modules:

- **B1 (scope).** Includes one frozen refined package. It succeeds only if it reduces off-scope H→E giving *and* is non-inferior on E→H and H→H giving; any welfare claim needs closed-loop validation.
- **B2 (memory).** No history versus the four joint prior actions, plus a same-partner versus different-partner factor. Reachable-state interventions are kept separate from prompt edits, which are framed as input sensitivity.
- **B3 (horizon).** Manipulates only the announced endpoint within valid intermediate states, including H–H states.
- **B4 (payoff table).** A symmetric, non-directive table with the same objective, and a comprehension probe in a separate call.

Analysis is clustered by base state and source seed. B also requires randomized invocation order, 5% hidden repeats, and a budget derived from states × conditions × replicates (≈ 6,050 calls).

## IV. Second model, money, free riding

- **Second model:** limited to four preselected fixed-state contrasts (≈ 1,200 calls). The claim boundary is the review's sentence ("these fixed-state behavioral effects replicate across a second model family"). The paper's Limitations say exactly this.
- **Money:** deprioritized. The paper now says the funnel locates *where* the sale fails (buyer non-payment) but not why, and makes no claim about monetary acceptance or pricing semantics.
- **Free riding:** optional. The endpoint is the paired estimator G_i = E[V_i(always-keep_i, π_−i) − V_i(π_i, π_−i)], not the deviator's rank, and no "no retaliation" prediction is made.

## V. Theoretical positioning

A new Related Work paragraph cites Akata et al. (2025), Fontana, Pierri & Aiello (2025), and CoopEval (Tewolde et al., 2026). It describes them accurately: Akata et al. use simplified payoff descriptions with neutral labels; Fontana et al. analyze comprehension, memory windows, and retaliation and build profiles from logs. The paragraph claims only the distinction the review proposes: VBE derives role-dependent local incentives from settlement rules rather than stating a matrix, and evaluates an announcement aimed at one relation on unnamed relations and on welfare. The paper does not claim that models "don't know which game they play", and it does not claim that log-based compliance profiles are novel.

Human studies (Selten & Stoecker 1986; Fischbacher, Gächter & Fehr 2001; Bicchieri 2006) are cited only as sources of dimensions to test: horizon, conditional cooperation, and empirical versus normative expectations. They are not a human baseline. The paper also states that a history effect could reflect reciprocity, imitation, or expected returns as well as norm compliance.
