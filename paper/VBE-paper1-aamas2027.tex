\begin{abstract}
Public natural-language rules are often evaluated as if compliance with the
named action were the mechanism that changes welfare.  We test that assumption
in the Verification Budget Economy, an eight-agent repeated-exchange
environment with scarce checks and intrinsically worthless transferable marks.
A byte-identical visible-announcement execution intervention shows that
blocking feasible Hard-to-Hard check giving lowers welfare within the gift arm
by 3.2083 points per account.  A prospectively
frozen seven-arm study replicates the gift package against no recommendation on
Hard-to-Hard swap rate ($+0.6070$) and welfare ($+2.0625$), but a zero-call
proposal audit finds that the named Easy-to-Hard relation rises only 0.123
above neutral, below a post-hoc 0.25 engagement screen.  Any-holder and Hard-partner
packages name Hard actors directly, whereas an engaged, mechanically harmful
Easy-to-Easy directive also raises Hard giving outside its named relation.  The
exact money package yields seller proposals but no executed sales, and its
named sale contrast does not clear that screen.  These results support
a layered conclusion: public packages can alter welfare-relevant role-
conditional proposal patterns and spill over beyond their named relation, but the
present designs do not identify helping-specific scope expansion, a priced-
semantic boundary, or a self-enforcing institution.  Proposal propagation,
physical execution, and welfare are therefore distinct empirical objects.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Public natural-language rules are increasingly used to coordinate language-model
teams, markets, and social simulations.  Yet an observed change in aggregate
outcomes does not reveal which part of the rule-to-outcome chain carried the
effect.  Agents may propose the named transaction, respond to an unmentioned
role relation, or produce proposals that the environment cannot execute.  A
study that records only surface compliance can therefore assign welfare to the
wrong mechanism.

We study this problem in the Verification Budget Economy (\vbe), an eight-
account, 24-round random-matching economy in which Hard agents benefit from a
partner's check, Easy agents can salvage an unused check, and transferable
marks have no direct or terminal value.  Public messages recommend gifting
checks or exchanging them for marks, while the engine executes only compatible
bilateral transitions.  This separation lets us ask three linked questions:
does a package engage the relation it names, which execution changes affect
welfare, and do explicit actor or partner restrictions constrain the
observed response?

We answer these questions in a staged design.  W-CO (welfare crowding-out)
first discovers a welfare
contrast between gift and money talk and exposes an unmentioned \hh channel
after the planned gift-versus-sale mechanism gate fails.  W-RG (welfare
role-generalization channel) then holds the
visible public announcement byte-identical and intervenes only after each
model response:
blocking feasible \hh giving lowers welfare by 3.2083 points per account, with
all 12 paired seeds moving in the same direction.  W-SGB (welfare semantic-
generalization boundary) subsequently varies
seven public-message packages under unchanged execution.  The gift package
replicates on \hh swap rate and welfare, but the zero-call proposal audit shows
that its named Easy-to-Hard relation does not clear the post-hoc screen;
any-holder and Hard-partner packages name Hard actors directly, while an
engaged harmful Easy--Easy directive spills into \hh behavior.  The money
package produces seller proposals but no executed sales.

The paper makes three contributions.  First, it estimates a welfare-relevant
execution contrast within the gift arm with a byte-identical visible-announcement
intervention rather than
inferring mechanism from the public wording.  Second, it provides a
role-conditional proposal map and a symmetric engagement audit that separates
literal support from candidate scope expansion.  Third, it establishes an
evidence discipline for language-mediated populations: proposal propagation,
physical execution, state feedback, and welfare must be measured as distinct
layers.  The resulting claim is about a causal systems property of a hosted
model population, not about private belief, common knowledge, or consciousness.

\section{Environment and Rule-to-Outcome Stack}

\subsection{Verification Budget Economy}

\vbe contains eight persistent accounts and runs for 24 rounds.  In each
round, controllers are assigned Hard or Easy roles and randomly matched with
meeting probability $q=0.4$.  A successful Hard task pays $R=3$.  A Hard
agent's own check succeeds with probability $p_H=0.32$; a partner-supplied
check succeeds with probability $p_P=0.93$.  At the start of each round,
each account receives its round endowment of $B=1$ check.  Every unused
inventory check converts to $v=0.5$ at round end and then expires.  Four marks
circulate but
have no direct or terminal payoff.  Replacing an own check with a partner check
increases expected Hard-task value by
\begin{equation}
R(p_P-p_H)=3(0.93-0.32)=1.83.
\end{equation}

Checks can move through a simultaneous exchange (a \emph{swap}), a one-way
\eh gift, or an Easy seller's mark-contingent transfer matched with a Hard
buyer's offered mark.  Controllers propose actions independently; the engine
executes only compatible joint transitions.  Mean final score per account is
the welfare outcome.  The principal mechanism outcome is the proportion of
\hh meetings that execute a mutual check swap.  Because treatment changes
holdings and future opportunities, the inferential unit is the complete
seed-level population trajectory, not a meeting.

Table~\ref{tab:payoffs} makes the transaction accounting explicit.  A received
check is recorded as a verification source for a Hard agent; it is not added
to the recipient's inventory.  In an E$\rightarrow$H gift or sale, however,
the Hard recipient retains its own check and can still receive the ordinary
end-of-round salvage.  The prompt exposes the current roles, both inventories, scores, and
the last four partner interactions.  A sale requires a seller's mark-contingent
proposal and a buyer's offered mark; a gift requires only the giver's
unconditional proposal.  These asymmetric acceptance conditions matter for
interpreting zero executed sales.

\subsection{Public rules as distributed policy programs}

The rule-to-outcome path is
\begin{equation}
\text{message}\rightarrow\text{proposal}\rightarrow\text{execution}
\rightarrow\text{state}\rightarrow\text{welfare}.
\end{equation}
Natural-language evaluations often observe the first two elements and infer
the rest.  \vbe records each layer.  We call a message a \emph{distributed
policy program} when it changes a population's mapping from role and local
state to proposed action.  This is an operational abstraction, not a claim
about an internal symbolic representation.

We annotate each directive $D$ as $(A_D,B_D,R_D,Q_D)$: named actor roles,
partner roles, transfer relation, and explicit restrictions such as ``only.''
This is a descriptive coding scheme, not a formal semantics.  A candidate
role spillover occurs outside its named actor--partner pairs; a change from a
one-way gift to a joint swap is a separate action-level distinction.
Confirmatory excess execution rates require both the declared effect-size
and error-control gates.  Semantic interpretation additionally needs engagement
of the named relation; our later proposal screen checks that prerequisite
descriptively.

Exact gift names Easy acting toward Hard, unlike the tested H--H pair.
The Hard-partner package allows Hard actors, so H--H fits its role predicate
even though a joint swap is not explicitly named.  Easy-only both names Easy
actors and prohibits Hard giving.  Its zero named E$\rightarrow$H proposals
and compound wording prevent isolation of actor-exclusivity semantics.

\section{Experimental Design}

\subsection{Discovery and execution intervention}

W-CO compares historical gift and money packages on 18 fresh paired seeds.
The welfare contrast was prospectively evaluated, but the \hh explanation was
found only after the planned behavioral-substitution gate failed.  W-CO is
therefore mechanism discovery, not confirmatory evidence for that channel.

W-RG converts the trace hypothesis into a prospective execution intervention.
Twelve fresh seeds each run four paired arms: neutral; standard gift; gift with
feasible \hh check giving suppressed after response; and gift with feasible
unconditional \eh giving suppressed after response.  The three gift arms have
byte-identical visible announcements and retain the current meeting's
structured proposal for the local execution transform.  They differ only in
that narrow post-response transform; subsequent proposals may respond to the
changed state.  Original intentions are retained because reciprocal Hard giving
can turn a candidate one-way \eh gift into a swap before realization.
The H--H filter sets \texttt{giveCheck=false} if both roles are Hard and
the proposer holds a check and proposes to give it.  The E$\rightarrow$H
filter applies the same assignment only to Easy givers facing Hard with
\texttt{requireChit=false}.  Both leave \texttt{giveChits} and
\texttt{requireChit} unchanged.  Models are queried separately in each arm.

\subsection{Prospective role-boundary test}

W-SGB keeps execution unmodified and randomizes seven message packages over 14
fresh paired seeds: neutral; exact gift; gift with the actor restricted to
``Easy only''; gift generalized to any check-holder; giving restricted only by
a Hard partner; exact money talk; and an Easy--Easy reciprocal-transfer
directive.  The last is an engaged negative control.  When two Easy agents
exchange checks, both lose the 0.5 salvage value of an otherwise retained
check, so the named execution destroys 1.0 total welfare.  A separate E--E swap
gate must pass before this arm can support an interpretation about scope.

Co-primary endpoints are seed-level \hh swap rate and mean welfare.  Each has
eight directional contrasts.  Holm adjustment controls familywise error at
0.025 within each endpoint family.  Minimum relevant effects are $+0.25$ for
swap rate and $+0.5$ for welfare.  We use MRES (minimum relevant effect size)
for these thresholds: the value is a practical-effect gate, so a small
significant difference cannot upgrade a scope or welfare claim.  The gift,
money, and neutral packages are byte-preserved anchors.  New packages are not
token- or length-matched; W-SGB identifies package-level scope profiles, not a
single word or parse.  After the frozen run, we borrowed the $0.25$ MRES for a
descriptive named-proposal engagement screen.  This post-hoc zero-call audit
qualifies interpretation; it is not another preregistered hypothesis family.  Zero executed
sales still cannot establish a priced-semantic boundary.

Table~\ref{tab:arms} shows the clauses that distinguish the packages.  Every
directive also states that it is a public statement rather than an engine rule,
may be followed or ignored, and that each agent should maximize its own score.
The supplement preserves the complete byte-exact messages.

Within each seed, arms share initial holdings, roles, pairings, and pregenerated
payoff randomness.  Cyclic orders and reversals place every arm twice in each
position and balance every pairwise precedence relation 7/7.  Strict schemas
prevent malformed outputs from becoming idle actions.  Each target study is
enclosed by an independently specified 14-call pre/post provider-health bracket
covering external controls, dominance anchors, prompt hashes, returned model,
live catalog, output diversity, entropy, and a metadata fingerprint.  W-RG and
W-SGB use canonical \texttt{deepseek-flash} in the same provider-labeled V4.1
Flash era.  Earlier \texttt{deepseek-v4-flash} results lack comparable returned
identities and are not pooled.  A bracket bounds an observed service interval;
it does not prove immutable weights.

This control was introduced after a serving incident in which 72 distinct
prompt hashes returned one identical action pattern.  A same-account,
same-harness probe on a then-distinct endpoint remained discriminative, and an
arithmetic suffix changed the output, showing that the prompt tail was read.
We classified that episode as a serving-identity failure rather than a
substantive behavioral null.  Provider health is consequently evaluated from
external controls and metadata independently of each target outcome.

\subsection{Estimands and inference}

W-RG estimates the paired dynamic total effect of changing an execution
contract under a byte-identical visible announcement, with the current
meeting's proposal held fixed only for that local execution transform.  W-SGB
estimates intention-to-treat effects of complete public-message packages under
the unchanged execution contract.  Both estimands include downstream feedback
through holdings, partner histories, and later responses; role and matching
schedules remain paired.  They do not decompose a
natural indirect effect and do not assume independence among agents or meetings.
This population-level treatment assignment follows causal work on interference:
when one agent's treatment changes other agents' exposures and outcomes,
unit-level no-interference estimands are generally unavailable without an
exposure model \cite{hudgens2008,aronow2017}.

Formally, let $Y_s(d,e)$ denote the seed-$s$ population outcome under public
directive package $d$ and execution policy $e$, where $s$ fixes the paired
initial state, role/matching schedule, and payoff draws.  The principal W-RG
contrast estimates
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{12}\sum_s\left[Y_s(d_{gift},e_{standard})-
Y_s(d_{gift},e_{block\text{-}HH})\right],
\end{equation}
whereas a W-SGB contrast estimates
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{14}\sum_s\left[Y_s(d_1,e_{standard})-
Y_s(d_0,e_{standard})\right].
\end{equation}
The first intervenes on execution after the current meeting's response while
allowing subsequent state feedback; it is not an open-loop replay or a natural-
mediation estimate.  The second intervenes on a visible package and allows
proposals to respond.  The two designs therefore answer different causal
questions and their estimates are not pooled.

For each contrast we compute an arm difference within every matched seed, then
average the paired differences.  Reported percentile intervals use 20,000
nonparametric resamples of the paired seed effects with a fixed analysis seed.
Directional $p$-values enumerate every sign assignment of the absolute paired
effects under a zero-centered sign-symmetry null.  W-SGB applies Holm adjustment
separately to its eight \hh and eight welfare hypotheses.  A gate passes only
when every frozen pair is present, the mean reaches its \mres, and the adjusted
$p$-value is at most 0.025.  Thus statistical significance below the declared
magnitude---as for gift minus negative---cannot upgrade the verdict.

W-RG completes 3,680 target calls and W-SGB 7,602, with zero retained API,
schema, or parse failures; their two pre/post brackets add 56 calls reported
separately.  Counts of meetings and transfers describe mechanisms, whereas
inference remains at the seed-level population unit.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Most candidate one-way gifts resolve as swaps}

In W-CO, gift talk exceeds money talk by 2.3333 mean score points (95\%
bootstrap CI $[1.7292,2.9306]$, one-sided exact $p=0.00001526$), but its planned
mechanism gate fails: the \eh gift-rate difference is only 0.03158, below the
0.25 threshold, and neither arm produces a mark sale.  Post-hoc traces instead
show \hh swaps rising from 27/154 under money talk to 138/154 under gift talk.
The discovery therefore identifies a welfare-relevant channel, not a clean
one-way-gift substitution.

W-RG then isolates that channel within the gift package (Table~\ref{tab:wrg}).
Standard gift produces 83 swaps in 107 \hh meetings; \hh blocking reduces the count to zero.
Suppressing \hh giving lowers welfare by 3.2083 points per account, with all 12
paired seeds positive.  Retaining \hh circulation while suppressing one-way
\eh realization exceeds the converse execution regime by 2.7604, again 12/12
positive.  Figure~\ref{fig:trace} contrasts the named \eh intention path with
the unmentioned \hh circulation and diagrams the post-response execution fork.

Low realized one-way gifting does not imply weak Easy intention.  Standard
gift contains 26 feasible original \eh gift intentions; 25 meet unconditional
Hard check-giving and resolve as swaps, leaving one realized one-way gift.
Most candidate one-way intentions are therefore absorbed into reciprocal
swaps in the realized trace; this describes the outcome pattern rather than a
mediation mechanism.  The \hh
intervention removes 102 Hard solves.  At three points per solve this accounts
for 306 aggregate points against 308 observed aggregate welfare points.  This
is dependency accounting, not an independent mediation estimate: blocking
changes later state, holdings, histories, and responses.  The identified
quantity is the dynamic total effect of disabling the \hh channel.  Because
W-RG has no neutral-with-\hh-blocked arm, it does not identify the fraction of
the gift-minus-neutral welfare effect carried by \hh circulation.  That
directive-by-execution interaction remains a planned follow-up.

\subsection{Gift replicates; helping specificity remains unresolved}

W-SGB completes all 98 cells.  Exact gift increases \hh swap rate over neutral
by 0.6070 and welfare by 2.0625; both are positive in 14/14 seeds and have
Holm-adjusted $p=0.0004883$ (Table~\ref{tab:wsgb}).  The gift reference thus
replicates on both endpoints.  Because neutral announces no recommendation,
this comparison estimates the total effect of the gift directive's presence
and content together; it is not by itself gift-specific.  The zero-call audit
in Table~\ref{tab:x0} then checks whether each package actually raises its
named proposal relation.  The same exact-gift arm also records 99
H$\rightarrow$E gifts (versus 3 in neutral); because each has negative direct
accounting, the package does not have a uniformly welfare-positive realized
relation.  Neutral itself contains 27/125 H--H swaps; the uptake timing is
descriptive and does not establish norm formation or monotone adoption.

The audit changes the scope interpretation.  Exact gift raises H--H giving
strongly, but its named E$\rightarrow$H proposal rate is only 0.1229, with a
seed-level mean difference of 0.1229 from neutral, below the 0.25 screen.  The
Easy-only package never proposes its named E$\rightarrow$H gift and therefore
cannot identify actor exclusivity.  Any-holder and Hard-partner packages clear
the proposal screen, but their actor support explicitly includes Hard, so their
H--H responses are inside the named support.  The harmful E--E package is both
engaged and outside its named E--E support when it raises H--H giving.  The
result is evidence that public packages alter role-conditional proposal
patterns and can produce engaged cross-role spillover; structured scope
expansion, role misbinding, and generic transfer priming remain observationally
equivalent.

As an environment-only calibration, we replayed the 14 seeds with scripted
policies.  Never transferring checks scored $54.241$ on average, first-best
Hard-directed transfer $63.188$, and always giving $60.813$.  A model-free
surrogate sampled from the exact-gift proposal map scored $56.683$; suppressing
its H-to-E component raised this to $57.853$.  These yardsticks are
descriptive, not new model evidence: they expose welfare-relevant waste
channels but do not identify why the model selected them.

\subsection{Near-total compliance with a harmful directive}

The negative directive adds no engine rule, reward, monitoring, or sanction,
and explicitly says that agents may ignore it.  Yet it executes 113 E--E swaps
in 114 opportunities.  Each named execution removes 0.5 points from each Easy
agent's private salvage and destroys 1.0 total welfare.  The zero-call proposal
audit reports a seed-level mean E--E gift-proposal rate of 0.991; the
corresponding pooled directed count is 227/228.  Separately, the frozen
execution engagement gate reports a seed-level mean E--E swap-rate contrast of
0.9821 (113/114 pooled meeting swaps), with 95\% CI $[0.9464,1.0000]$ and
adjusted $p=0.0000610$.  These are distinct proposal and execution metrics.
Despite naming only E--E transactions, the directive raises \hh swap rate over
neutral by 0.3777.  The payoff table shows a direct cost of $1.0$ per E--E
swap, but this design cannot tell whether agents knowingly accept that cost or
fail to represent the salvage consequence.  The latter question is reserved
for a payoff-salience replay probe.

Near-total named compliance therefore does not establish welfare alignment.
The same package also induces potentially useful \hh circulation, and its net

No W-SGB arm yields an executed mark sale, although E$\rightarrow$H
seller proposals are present.  Descriptively, its \hh effect is

$-0.2054$ ($[-0.3393,-0.0714]$) relative to neutral.  The registered tests were
one-sided for positive effects, so these negative directions were not
prospectively tested and are not upgraded.  They motivate an
engagement-gated replication of possible suppression, but cannot distinguish
``priced-exchange semantics do not expand'' from a failure to match seller and
buyer proposals.  The current data therefore support no priced-semantic
boundary claim.

\subsection{Boundary arms do not isolate actor exclusivity}

The ``Easy only'' arm has mean \hh swap rate 0.237, near neutral's 0.223, and
mean welfare 0.049 below neutral.  Exact gift exceeds it by 0.5923 in swap rate
and 2.1116 in welfare, clearing both frozen gates.  Descriptively, the Easy-only
variant remains near neutral, but its explicit prohibition and zero named
E$\rightarrow$H proposal rate prevent a quantifier-only interpretation.  By
contrast, ``any check-holder'' and ``only when the partner is Hard'' retain
strong \hh circulation and welfare effects; the latter explicitly includes

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What the evidence identifies}

Taken together, the studies identify a layered causal object rather than a
single semantic effect.  W-RG shows that, within the gift arm, changing the
post-response execution contract to block feasible \hh giving lowers welfare by
3.2083 points per account.  Because no neutral-with-\hh-blocked arm was run,
this dynamic total effect does not identify the share of the gift-versus-neutral
contrast carried by \hh circulation or establish necessity.  W-SGB
shows that the same package changes \hh behavior relative to neutral, but the
zero-call audit prevents us from treating that response as a demonstrated
helping-specific expansion, because the named \eh relation does not clear the
post-hoc screen.  The any-holder and Hard-partner packages name Hard actors
directly, and the harmful E--E package demonstrates engaged spillover without a
helping interpretation.  These alternatives remain observationally distinct in
meaning but not in the present data.

The best-supported abstraction is therefore a \emph{distributed policy program}:
public text changes a population's mapping from local roles and state to
proposals, and the engine determines which proposals become state transitions.
This is more than an ignored string, but less than a social institution with
common belief, legitimacy, consent, stable financing, or open-ended equilibrium
support.  Reserving ``institution'' for that stronger object keeps the causal
finding useful without assigning the model population beliefs or legitimacy
that were not measured.

\subsection{Implications for multi-agent system design}

The design implication is an audit requirement.  Natural-language rules should
be versioned like code: systems should log exact text, actor and beneficiary
scope, model-service identity, proposal output, execution contract, and welfare
transition.  Evaluations should first test whether the named relation engages,
then probe unmentioned interactions with an engaged undesirable directive;
otherwise a helping-specific interpretation can survive because its comparator
did nothing.  Proposal and execution should remain separate audit layers, and
the final report should distinguish named compliance, cross-role spillover,
realized transitions, state feedback, and welfare.  Optimizing only the first
two can select a rule that is legible and behaviorally contagious while still
inducing a per-transaction loss; aggregate welfare damage must be established
separately.

\section{Limitations}

The primary results concern one hosted endpoint, one provider-labeled era, and
one finite-horizon economy.  Temperature zero and healthy pre/post brackets do
not establish immutable weights or cross-model generality.  W-SGB identifies
whole-message packages rather than tokens or parses.  The unengaged money arm
cannot locate a priced-semantic boundary; one frozen repair attempt ended
incomplete after a provider transport failure and yields no inference.
The only cross-family concordance is three unpaired, unbracketed historical
grok-4.5 seeds and is too weak to count as replication.  More usefully, that
earlier interface produced 24 mark sales in 44 opportunities while the current
one produces none, demonstrating context dependence without identifying an era
effect.

Treatment changes population trajectories, so seed-level runs are the units and
the work does not identify natural-mediation shares.  The economy fixes price,
mark supply, horizon, and task technology, omitting issuance, price discovery,
counterfeiting, currency competition, and participant-funded enforcement.
Frozen account-level summaries are exploratory diagnostics only and do not
identify fairness or legitimacy.  The studies observe public messages, proposals,
executions, and outcomes, not private belief, common knowledge, consent, or
consciousness.
Protocols were hash-frozen locally before target calls but not externally
registered.  The H--H endpoint was selected after W-CO's mechanism failure;
fresh-seed prospective tests assess this selected phenotype, not the original
mechanism hypothesis or an unselected program-wide claim.  Future work should
use fresh seeds and matched directive-force,
reciprocity, actor-scope, beneficiary-scope, and engagement-gated price variants,
then replicate on a verifiably distinct model family.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Institutional statements and operative rules.}
Institutional grammar decomposes rules into attributes, deontic operators,
aims, conditions, and sanctions \cite{crawford1995}; formal accounts distinguish
institutional power from permission and physical possibility \cite{jones1996},
and constitutive rules from regulative norms \cite{boella2004}.  Normative
multi-agent-systems work then represents role-indexed obligations, permissions,
monitoring, enforcement, and institutional change explicitly
\cite{esteva2002,fornara2008,king2015}.  These traditions specify what a rule
means or authorizes.  \vbe asks a complementary execution question: when the
rule is natural language interpreted by model policies, do its realized actor,
beneficiary, action, and condition scopes match the written ones?  Separating
model proposal from engine execution operationalizes the distinction between a
normative statement and a state transition.

\paragraph{Institutions, conventions, and norm emergence.}
Accounts of convention and common knowledge separate shared observation from
higher-order epistemic states \cite{lewis1969,rubinstein1989}, while experiments
study spontaneous convention formation and tipping \cite{centola2015,centola2018}.
Social-law synthesis studies top-down constraints \cite{shoham1992}; work on
norm emergence studies bottom-up convergence through social learning,
interaction, propagation, and norm life cycles
\cite{sen2007,savarimuthu2007,savarimuthu2011,morrismartin2019}.  \vbe's public
artifact is top-down and its response is immediate, so we do not label it
bottom-up emergence, internalization, or common belief.  Searle's collective
status functions and Ostrom's participant-fitted governance similarly set a
higher bar for ``institution'' than behavioral compliance alone
\cite{searle2005,ostrom1990}.

\paragraph{Generative social simulation.}
Agent-based computational social science explains macro-patterns by specifying
interacting actors and generating population dynamics \cite{macy2002,epstein2006}.
LLM work extends this program to believable agents, simulated human samples,
replications of human-subject studies, and interactive social-intelligence
evaluation \cite{park2023,aher2023,argyle2023,zhou2024,anthis2025}.  Concordia
provides a componentized generative agent-based modeling framework with a
grounded action resolver \cite{vezhnevets2023concordia}; economic simulations
such as Homo Silicus and EconAgent use language models to instantiate
heterogeneous economic actors and market dynamics
\cite{horton2023homosilicus,li2024econagent}.  These systems establish the
usefulness of language-mediated simulation, but they do not by themselves
identify whether a public rule engages its named relation before a macro-level
outcome is interpreted.  Our target is therefore not human fidelity or
believability, but a causal systems property of a hosted model population:
how a versioned public artifact changes proposals, executable transitions,
cross-role spillovers, and welfare under a fixed engine.

\paragraph{Language-model collectives.}
Recent studies examine coordination, cooperation, convention formation, legal
structures, and economic interaction in LLM populations
\cite{ren2024,agashe2025,flint2025,zhu2025,madmoun2026,qi2026,wang2026}.
GovSim studies sustainable cooperation in a common-resource society, and
Welfare Diplomacy benchmarks general-sum cooperation under strategic pressure
\cite{piatti2024govsim,mukobi2023welfarediplomacy}.  These benchmarks make
collective welfare and failure modes visible, but their aggregate outcomes do
not separate named proposal engagement from the execution channel that carries
the outcome.
The naming-game debate further shows that collective behavior can be
observationally compatible with training-data reproduction
\cite{barrie2025,flintreply2025}.  We therefore do not infer emergence from
novelty.  Our contribution is an identification design: a byte-identical
execution intervention locates a welfare-relevant contrast within the gift arm,
and an engaged
harmful control tests cross-role spillover without equating surface compliance
with mechanism or value.

\paragraph{Causal effects in interacting populations.}
General-interference frameworks make outcomes functions of other units'
treatments or exposures and require estimands matched to that dependence
\cite{hudgens2008,aronow2017}.  In \vbe, early actions change holdings, later
partners' opportunities, and future prompts, so the inferential unit is the
paired seed-level population trajectory rather than a meeting or account.  This
supports dynamic total effects, not individual direct effects or natural
mediation shares.  Correlated-equilibrium theory formalizes how shared
information can coordinate pure actions without explicit randomization
\cite{aumann1974subjectivity,aumann1987correlated}; our design does not infer a
common prior or equilibrium.  Search-theoretic monetary models motivate the matching and
token environment \cite{kiyotaki1989,kiyotaki1993}, but fixed supply, horizon,
and prices preclude a monetary-equilibrium claim.  These causal and economic
frameworks motivate our separation of proposal, execution, and welfare, but the
contribution here is a population-level identification design rather than a
training or calibration method.

\section{Conclusion}

A public sentence can change a language-model population without directly
specifying the welfare-carrying transition.  In \vbe, a byte-identical
visible-announcement execution intervention shows that blocking feasible \hh
giving lowers welfare by 3.2083 points per account within the gift arm; without
a neutral-with-blocked comparison, this does not establish a necessary channel
or a mediation share for the gift-versus-neutral effect.  The proposal audit
shows that the named \eh relation fails the post-hoc screen, so no
helping-specific expansion claim is identified.  The harmful E--E directive
spills into \hh behavior, while Easy-only remains near neutral and cannot
identify actor exclusivity; money is unengaged and sets no priced-semantic
boundary.  The contribution is an audit rule: separate proposal, execution,
feedback, and welfare.
